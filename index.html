What is Fiber?

"Fiber is type of carbohydrate that the body can't digest. Though most carbohydrates are broken down into sugar molecules called glucose, fiber cannot be broken down into sugar molecules, and instead it passes through the body undigested. Fiber helps regulate the body's use of sugars, helping to keep hunger and blood sugar in check. Children and adults need at least 25 to 35 grams of fiber per day for good health, but most Americans get only about 15 grams a day. Great sources are whole grains, whole fruits and vegetables, nuts, legumes like black beans, lentils, and chickpeas."

Source: Harvard Nutrition Source

But Beans Make Me Gassy! What Do I Do?

Sudden increase in fiber intake, especially fiber-packed foods like legumes: black beans, chickpeas, and lentils can cause discomfort in your stomach, and even some experience painful gassy pressure in their gut. However that is usually in the first week when high fiber diet is introduced, after the second week the discomfort lessens and in a few weeks time, there are no side effects at all. It takes time for the body to adapt to the higher fiber intake. Therefore, if you encounter these issues, try these easy steps:

  • Gradually increase the intake of legumes: start with 10g a day, and increase another 10g the next week until you reach the recommended 35g per day.
  • Introduce regular movement: Taking walks for 10-15 minutes after meal can encourage bowel movement and digestion.
  • Drink More Water: Drink 2 Liters or more water (about 3-4 water bottle worth) a day. Fiber absorbs water, so replenish yourself to avoid dehydration.
  • Soak and Rinse Legumes: If you cooking raw legumes, make sure to prep them by soaking and then thoroughly rinsing them. For canned legumes, rinse them thoroughly before use.

Source: Grains & Legumes Nutrition Council
